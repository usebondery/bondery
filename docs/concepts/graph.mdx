The graph shows your people and the lines between them. Use it when you want to see who knows whom, who shares a workplace or a school, and which people sit in the same group.

## What you see

Each person is a photo. You sit in the middle, with a line to every other person on the view. That line means the person is in your account. It is not a relationship you wrote down.

A relationship you saved draws its own line between two people. Every saved line stays on the graph.

A company or a school appears when at least two people share it. Past jobs count, and so does every school on a profile. The mark is a rounded logo, with a line to each person who belongs there.

A group is a soft circle behind its members. People in a group appear even when they have no saved relationship and no shared company or school. Circles overlap when one person is in more than one group.

On a phone, the same links are short sentences. The picture is there when the screen is wide enough.

## Move around

- Drag the background to pan. Drag a person, a company, or a school to move that mark.
- Use the zoom buttons, or scroll, to move closer or farther. **1×** is the top button and puts the view back to 100%. The bottom button centers you in the view.
- Search from the field at the top. Names that do not match fade. The lines stay.

## Focus

Rest the pointer on a person. That person stays fully lit. The people, companies, and schools tied to them stay lit, a little softer, so you can tell the one under the pointer from the rest. Everything else fades.

A company or a school works the same way. That mark stays strongest. Its people stay a step quieter.

Click a person to keep the preview open. The preview shows the name, the headline, and the location. The graph card covers any part that crosses its edge. A panel lists that person's relationships. Double-click a person to open the full page.

From a person's page, **Show on graph** opens the graph with that person in view.

## A large account

The graph draws up to 500 people, 1,000 relationships, and 40 companies or schools.
